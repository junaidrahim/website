# Writing principles

## Timeless catalogue over viral output

Prefer work that remains useful several years later. Do not shape titles or arguments around short-term engagement.

## Iteration over indefinite polish

A good post that ships is better than a perfect post that remains in a backlog. Surface stale drafts and ask what makes the smallest version publishable.

## Depth over breadth

Prefer one concrete mechanism to a survey. Use actual code, plans, protocols, measurements, and failure modes. Remove generic framing.

## Technical but accessible

Write for technical readers who do not share Junaid's local context. Define specialized terms on first use; do not over-explain standard engineering terms.

## Real work, safely abstracted

Keep mechanisms and lessons from real work while removing private names and identifying details. Do not publish internal strategy, customer information, unreleased products, confidential data, or private figures.

## Titles, openings, and voice

Use sentence case. Avoid clickbait, listicle padding, emojis, filler, generic AI cadence, and “In this post, we will explore.” Start with the mechanism, observation, incident, or argument. Use first person when it adds evidence or responsibility. Be confident without being pompous. Above all, do not rewrite Junaid's voice.
